# Trial brief

You are an agent asked to make and present a short deck with one presentation tool. The same brief, in the same
words, goes to every tool in the trial. Work only in your working folder. Use the tool's own documentation and its
own commands, and nothing else about the tool. Report what you did and what went wrong, honestly.

## The deck

Six scenes. Use these words exactly where they are in quotes.

1. Title. Heading "Channel B now leads demand". One line under it: "A five-minute walk through the numbers, the method, and what to check next."
2. Claim with a bar chart. Heading "Channel B leads demand, 60 to 40". A bar chart with two bars, Channel A = 40 and Channel B = 60, labelled "Units sold, Q3 (thousands)". A source line: "Source: Q3 demand survey, n = 1,204".
3. Method. Heading "How we measured it". Three points: "Survey of 1,204 buyers in September", "Weighted to the sales mix by region", "Demand means stated intent within 90 days".
4. Risks. Heading "Three things could change the picture, and each one is worth a conversation before anyone acts on the Channel B number on the second slide". Three points: "The sample leans toward web buyers", "Stated intent is not purchase", "Q4 promotions may move both channels".
5. Next steps. Heading "What to check next". Three numbered points: "Re-weight the sample by store traffic", "Compare stated intent with Q3 orders", "Repeat the survey after the Q4 promotions".
6. Sources. Heading "Sources". Two points: "Q3 demand survey, internal, n = 1,204", "Q3 orders export, finance, October".

Every scene has one sentence of speaker notes that the presenter can read but the audience does not see. Write any sentence you like.

## The five tasks

Do them in order. After each one, stop and write down what you did, the commands you ran, and anything that failed.

1. Create. Build the deck above.
2. Revise. The survey was re-weighted: Channel A is 45 and Channel B is 55. Change the chart and make the heading on scene 2 say "Channel B leads demand, 55 to 45". Change nothing else.
3. Check. Use the tool's own checks, if it has any, to find anything that does not fit on its slide, and fix it without losing the meaning.
4. Deliver. Make a folder that someone with no network, no Node and no account can open by double-clicking a file and present with the keyboard. The speaker notes must stay available to the presenter.
5. Recover. Someone else will edit scene 2's heading while you hold an older copy. A script runs this step after you finish, so you only say how you would find out that your copy was out of date.

Stop a task after 40 tool calls or if you are blocked, and say so. Do not search the internet except the tool's own documentation site.
